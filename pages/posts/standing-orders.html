We are not interested in who can click faster. We are extremely interested in who was right earlier.

The problem with APM

Actions per minute is a real skill, and it is a real skill in the same way that being able to type quickly is a real skill for a novelist. It correlates with outcomes without being what the game is about.

More practically: a game where mechanical execution decides matches has a very short strategic ceiling, because the optimal strategy is whatever survives being executed sloppily under time pressure.

What we did instead

Standing orders. You give a formation a job and a condition, and it keeps doing that job without you. Screen this ridge. Withdraw below forty percent. Interdict supply, ignore combat.

You can still micro. Micro is better than a standing order, in the specific fight you are watching. You just cannot watch every fight, and the game is now about choosing which one deserves you.

The visibility rule

Every standing order is visible to your opponent when they can see the unit. We considered hiding them and decided against it: an RTS should not have information asymmetry the interface created rather than the players.
